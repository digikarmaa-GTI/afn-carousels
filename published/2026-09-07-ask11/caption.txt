Someone asked on Reddit this week: what is the first step a comms person should actually take for a nonprofit? If the job is more than newsletters and a social calendar, what is the real first move?

Best question I have seen in months.

Here is my answer, and I paid for it before I understood it. I once sent thousands of emails in a single week. Every one was correct. Every one was on brand. Almost nobody wrote back. Posting is not the same thing as being known.

A stranger can do your posting. A stranger cannot sound like your organization, because a stranger has never met the people you serve.

Only 18% of nonprofits say AI is used across their team workflows. 7% say it is written into their goals, budgets and performance measures. Everyone else is doing it one prompt at a time, starting from nothing every morning. (Virtuous 2026 Nonprofit AI Adoption Report, 346 organizations.)

Five things to do before you post again:

1. Write down who the organization actually helps.
2. Collect ten sentences a real supporter has said.
3. Put both into the tool's memory. Once.
4. Ask it what it still had to guess about you.
5. Then post. It will sound like you, not everyone.

The first step is not a post. It is a setup.
